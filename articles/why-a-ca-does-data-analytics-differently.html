Why a Chartered Accountant does data analytics differently

A data analyst finds patterns. A finance executive knows which patterns matter. That combination changes the kind of analysis a business actually gets.

Two different starting points

A typical data analyst starts with the data: what tables exist, what's clean, what can be joined. A typical finance executive starts with the business: what decisions are coming up, what could go wrong, what would change if margin dropped two points. Both starting points are useful. Few people have both.

Knowing which number is lying to you

Financial data has quirks that aren't obvious unless you've closed a set of books. Revenue recognised in the wrong period. Accruals that smooth out a bad month. A "profitable" client that's only profitable because overhead was allocated generously. A data scientist without financial training can build a technically correct model on numbers that are misleading the whole business. A Chartered Accountant recognises the quirk before it becomes the headline finding.

Asking the question a board would ask

Years spent in CEO and CFO roles change the questions you ask of an analysis. Not just "what does the data show," but "would I act on this," "what's the downside if I'm wrong," and "does this hold up if someone challenges it in an exco meeting." That commercial instinct is hard to teach and easy to miss in a purely technical analysis.

Where this actually shows up

In practice it looks like: profitability analysis that correctly allocates shared costs instead of ignoring them. Forecasts that reflect how the business actually collects cash, not just how revenue is booked. Dashboards built around the metrics management is accountable for, not just the metrics that were easiest to pull. Small differences, but they're the difference between an analysis that's interesting and one that changes a decision.

Why this matters for a growing business

A growing business usually can't afford — and doesn't yet need — a full finance team and a full data team. What it needs is someone who can move between the two without a translation layer in between. That's the specific gap this combination fills.
