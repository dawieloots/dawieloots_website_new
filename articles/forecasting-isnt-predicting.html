Forecasting isn't predicting the future. It's preparing for it.

A better way to think about forecasts, scenarios and uncertainty.

The forecast will be wrong

Every serious forecast is wrong. That isn't a failure of forecasting. It is a property of the future. The purpose is to create a structured view of what could happen based on what we know today.

A forecast is a model of assumptions

Revenue growth, customer volumes, prices, costs, headcount and collections are assumptions translated into numbers. One of the biggest benefits is forcing the business to make those assumptions explicit.

Scenarios are often more useful

Instead of asking “What will happen?”, ask what happens if things go roughly as expected, revenue is 10% lower, a major customer leaves, costs rise faster than expected, or a hiring plan starts earlier.

Probability can add another layer

Where there is enough evidence, scenarios can be assigned probabilities. But this should not create false precision. The quality of the assumptions matters more than the number of decimal places.

The management value

A good forecast creates an early-warning system. If cash may become tight in three months, you want to know now. Forecasting is valuable because it gives management time to act while there is still something to act on.
